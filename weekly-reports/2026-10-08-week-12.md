## Goal and reason for this experiment

Sir questioned using a greedy/perfect-looking source sequence too early.
We therefore tested the input-driven burning program with a **hand-picked,
non-optimized** legal sequence: vertex 1 in round 1, vertex 9 in round 2,
and vertex 5 in round 3 on the same nine-vertex undirected path. This was
chosen by us, not by the optional Python source-search tool. It is not a
claim of statistical randomness or optimality. The aim was to see whether
the Giraph computation behaves correctly even when the selected sources
do **not** burn the whole graph within three rounds.

## Result and explanation

```text
1  B   1
2  B   2
3  B   3
4  NB -1
5  B   3
6  NB -1
7  NB -1
8  B   3
9  B   2
```

Round 1 burns source 1. In round 2, fire from 1 reaches 2 while source 9
ignites. In round 3, fire from 2 reaches 3, fire from 9 reaches 8, and
source 5 ignites. Vertices 4, 6 and 7 are still unburned after the **third
and final** round, so they show `NB -1`. The output matches the calculation
we wrote **before** running Giraph. It demonstrates correctness of this
small experiment, not a proof of large-scale performance.

The Python preflight checker predicted burn rounds for comparison but did
not select the sources. The output formatter turned Giraph's numeric state
into `B`/`NB`. For code and command meanings in one place, use
`docs/WEEK-11-INPUT-DRIVEN-BURNING-SELF-CONTAINED-STUDY.md`, which now
includes this follow-up.
