# Week 14 — 16–22 October 2026 (work recorded 18 October)

The next supervisor meeting is planned for Thursday 22 October. Before that
meeting, our team revisited the notebook example and the message that the
Graph Burning exercise should first be made easier to read in Python. We
tested one precise interpretation: with three chosen sources, source 1 can
select at most two direct neighbours, source 4 at most one, and source 6
none. We have labelled this **source-only limited-neighbour burning**. It
is not the same as standard graph burning's two-hop neighbourhood.

## What we made and why

- The two-part eight-vertex text graph in
  [`datasets/limited-neighbour-burning-demo/`](../datasets/limited-neighbour-burning-demo/)
  gives source 1 three neighbours. This lets us check that it actually picks
  only two, instead of confusing the restriction with a node that has only
  two neighbours to begin with.
- The hand-written `schedule-1-4-6.txt` names the sources and exact selected
  neighbours: `1 1 2 3`, `2 4 5`, `3 6 -`. No greedy or random selection
  program was used.
- [`scripts/simulate_limited_neighbour_burning.py`](../scripts/simulate_limited_neighbour_burning.py)
  reads every graph part, checks the data and schedule, applies limits
  `2,1,0`, prints each round, and reports `B`/`NB` with first burn round.
  It is a **local Python** simulation, not a Giraph job.
- [`tests/test_limited_neighbour_burning.py`](../tests/test_limited_neighbour_burning.py)
  checks the hand-calculated answer, the cap, invalid choices, and the
  deliberate lack of automatic propagation. All seven tests passed.
